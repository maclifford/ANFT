The three invitations

Standardized for every event, worded as invitations rather than form fields. The middle one is phrased to pair with a post-event survey, so hopes stated beforehand can be revisited afterward.

What is calling you to this training?

What are you hoping for — your intention for this time?

Is there anything about how you learn best, or anything you’d like us to know to support you?

The third is optional and kept open rather than a checklist, so it doesn’t actively solicit health or disability details — the lightest posture under data-protection rules.

The two checkboxes

Payment & refund policy
“I have read and understand the payment & refund policy,” linked to that page. In the payment modal, required before paying.
Storage & privacy consent
Explicit consent to store and share responses with guides, linked to the privacy policy. In the introduction modal, required before submitting. policy to be updated first

Why an introduction at all

Four purposes, in priority order: to help people feel seen and met; to give guides a read on what motivates each participant; to capture a pre-event snapshot of hopes that a later survey can be measured against; and to surface learning styles, needs, or requests. The first purpose lives less in the questions than in the reply — the confirmation and the guides’ actual reading of what was written are what make it land.

Data, storage & consent — sequencing

Because responses are stored durably and can touch sensitive ground, three things happen in order before the submit step goes live:

  • The privacy policy is written/updated first — the consent checkbox has to point at something real.
  • A storage provider is chosen with advice, on two criteria: willingness to sign a data-processing agreement, and where the data physically lives.
  • Only then is the submit endpoint wired to that storage.

Likely-relevant regimes given the international audience: GDPR / UK GDPR, Canada’s PIPEDA, California’s CPRA. HIPAA most likely does not apply (ANFT isn’t a healthcare provider billing insurance), so HIPAA-grade storage is probably not the bar. This is a note, not legal advice — worth confirming with counsel.
